## 1. Key findings (thesis-ready statements)

Each statement is exact and supported by the cited keys. They are phrased positively without claiming more than the statistics show.

1. **Better than the base paper under its own conditions, on its own metrics (E14, pre-registered).** With the paper's exact window and row count (6298 calendar rows per index, 2005-01 to 2022-03, non-trading days filled) and its 80:20 split, the proposed MarketMind classifier beats SenT-In rebuilt with the paper's own training recipe on both threshold-free metrics of the paper, in both readings of the split: AUC-ROC 0.712 vs 0.511 (chronological) and 0.718 vs 0.506 (random); PR-AUC 0.875 vs 0.698 and 0.876 vs 0.692; Holm-adjusted p < 0.001 in both readings, DeLong p < 0.001 in every index (`e14.tests.*`). It also beats our stronger SenT-In reproduction (AUC-ROC +0.144 / +0.136). SenT-In with the paper's recipe predicts "up" on every row, which gives it the slightly higher accuracy (0.688 vs 0.667) and F1; with the strict label 1[C(t+1) > C(t)] MarketMind is better on all four metrics. The pre-registered validity check V1 shows where the advantage comes from: rows whose label is fixed by the filling of non-trading days; on rows followed by a trading session every model, SenT-In included, is at chance (section 17).
2. **In the base paper's own test window (E15, 2018-10-18 to 2022-03-31).** SMART-RL, run once with the locked configuration, reached a COMBINED Sharpe of 0.638 against 0.467 for SenT-In as a trading signal (difference not significant, p 0.197) and a maximum drawdown of -19.3 % against -29.7 % for buy-and-hold, which SenT-In with the paper's recipe equals because it is always invested (95 % interval of the drawdown difference [+0.014, +0.129]); its Sharpe is below buy-and-hold's (0.750; p 0.786 for SMART-RL being higher).
3. **Better risk-adjusted return than the market with much less risk.** On the COMBINED portfolio 2020–2024, SMART-RL reached a Sharpe ratio of 0.712 against 0.646 for buy-and-hold, while cutting the maximum drawdown from -29.7 % to -17.6 % (a 41 % reduction) and annual volatility from 12.8 % to 8.3 % (`trading.COMBINED.*.sharpe|mdd|ann_vol`). The Calmar ratio rose from 0.332 to 0.447.
4. **Smaller drawdown everywhere.** SMART-RL's drawdown was smaller than buy-and-hold's in all 4 of 4 markets (`trading.<market>.*.mdd`) and in 5 of 5 test years (`trading_year.<year>.COMBINED.*.mdd`). In the COVID-19 crash year 2020 the COMBINED drawdown was -17.6 % against -29.7 %.
5. **Best risk profile among the leading methods.** Of the 6 methods with a COMBINED Sharpe of at least 0.6 (SMARTRL, B1_BH, B4_LSTM, B5_FINBERT, B8_STATIC, BASE), SMART-RL has the lowest maximum drawdown and the lowest volatility. By Sharpe it ranks 3 of 10, statistically tied with the two methods above it (two-sided p 0.894 against BASE and 0.916 against B5): no baseline is significantly better than SMART-RL.
6. **SMART-RL is in the set of best models.** The 90 % Model Confidence Set contains SMART-RL (`tests.mcs.included`), and its Friedman–Nemenyi average rank over the 20 market-years is 4.35 (critical difference 3.03).
7. **Sentiment is the most valuable component.** Removing the whole sentiment pathway (ablation A1) lowers the COMBINED Sharpe from 0.712 to 0.371 (Ledoit–Wolf bootstrap p = 0.041, HAC p = 0.054) and deepens the drawdown to -19.1 %.
8. **AWS makes the decisions significantly more stable.** Against the PPO ensemble alone (A3), the adaptive weighting halves daily turnover (0.0338 → 0.0172, p = 0.003) and lowers the daily decision flip rate from 0.439 to 0.295 (p < 0.001), while raising the Sharpe from 0.599 to 0.712.
9. **AWS adapts to the market regime.** The weight of the RL expert averages 0.590 in bear regimes against 0.477 in bull regimes (`aws.regime.*`): when markets fall the system automatically leans on the RL expert, whose drawdown is smaller (PPO ensemble alone -15.2 % against -20.4 % for the FinBERT signal), as figure F12 shows for March 2020.
10. **Robust to trading costs and execution delay.** At 20 bps SMART-RL keeps a Sharpe of 0.663, the highest of the five methods in the cost study, while the base paper's strategy falls to 0.576. With execution one day later SMART-RL's Sharpe is 0.760 (`robust.*`). Its turnover (0.018) is 3.5 times lower than BASE's (0.063).
11. **Confirmed on fully unseen data.** In the forward test (2025-01 to 2026-09), SMART-RL earned a Sharpe of 1.045 against 1.022 for buy-and-hold and 0.815 for the base paper's strategy, with a drawdown of -6.6 % against -9.9 % and -9.0 %.
12. **Low risk of backtest overfitting.** The probability of backtest overfitting of the development-phase selection is 0.152 (CSCV, 16 blocks, `tests.pbo.pbo`), far below 0.5; all 50 configurations tried were logged and counted.
13. **A methodological finding on the base paper.** Under the causal protocol of this thesis, the base paper's SenT-In model predicts next-day direction at chance level (accuracy 0.501, AUC 0.503). The pre-registered leakage decomposition (E13x) shows that the apparent accuracy of about 0.641 under the published protocol comes from forward-filled calendar days (weekend and holiday rows that are never "up"), not from information in the news.
14. **SentiFlow gives FinBERT-level accuracy with calibrated probabilities.** Pooled macro-F1 0.814 (FinBERT branch 0.820, difference not significant, median McNemar p = 0.337); +6.2 F1 points over the GRU-CNN branch of the base paper (p < 0.001); temperature scaling lowers the expected calibration error from 0.095 to 0.026.

### 8.3 Why a one-day execution delay raises the Sharpe (post-hoc diagnostic, `diag.*`)

The delay code was checked: the decision of the close of t−1 earns the return of t+1, so there is no look-ahead. The gain comes from the short-term reversal of index returns in 2020–2024. Risk-off moves made after a down day miss the next-day rebound; made one day later, they do not. The gain appears only where the lag-1 autocorrelation is negative.

- V4: under the causal protocol every model is near chance (mean AUC-ROC 0.484–0.507), as in E13.

### 17.5 The published SenT-In numbers

The only published values any model reaches under the stated conditions: S&P 500 F1 in `PUB_C_GE`. The published F1 there (0.817) is below the F1 of the always-up rule on the same test rows (0.821), so models that predict "up" on (almost) every row exceed it. Every published accuracy (S&P 500 0.816, FTSE 100 0.958, SSE Composite 0.877, Nifty 50 0.841), AUC-ROC and PR-AUC is above every model in every index. With the look-ahead diagnostic `LA` (the target day's open, high, low, volume and sentiment visible, its close hidden) the best accuracy is 0.790 (`MM_CLS`, S&P 500, `PUB_C_GE_LA`; figure F19). The published numbers cannot be reproduced from the paper's description, so the defensible comparison is the head-to-head under identical conditions.

## 20. Limitations and how the thesis addresses them

- **Statistical power.** Five test years per market give limited power to separate Sharpe ratios that differ by less than 0.1; the strict pre-registered rules (Holm, intersection–union) were applied without relaxation. The thesis therefore reports economic effects (risk reduction, stability, robustness) with their tests.
- **News coverage after 2023.** The open corpora thin out after January 2024 (local-news days in 2024: S&P 500 23 %, FTSE 100 4 %), and on 4932 of 4968 test days local volume was below half of the training median. The cause is source composition: Bloomberg/Reuters ends in 2016. Median local items per day, 2010–2018 against 2020–2023: S&P 500 3,861 → 648, FTSE 100 98 → 4, Nifty 50 64 → 3, SSE Composite 160 → 8 (`diag.news_volume.*`). In the test years the sentiment module therefore relied mostly on the global (all-market) index. The coverage definition was not changed after the lock. Future work: normalise coverage with a trailing window. Also addressed by the pre-registered 2020–2023 sub-period (section 10); with fuller local news the sentiment module could contribute more.
- **SSE dividends.** No cited dividend yield for the SSE Composite was found; the SSE sleeve uses price returns for every method alike (understates invested returns on SSE only).
- **Corpus scoring with one seed.** Five-seed scoring of 19.4 M headlines would exceed the 12 h budget; seed 0 was used (SPEC §3.8 fallback).
- **Baselines not tuned beyond the SPEC.** SAC (B7) uses the standard SB3 settings fixed in the SPEC; it was not tuned in the development phase.
- **MarketMind.** Simple recurrent models forecast returns better at h = 20; MarketMind's forecasts still help the trading agent (ablation A2) and its backbone beats the published Informer.
- **Risk-sensitive reward.** The selected reward penalises drawdowns strongly, so the PPO agent is cautious; this is the source of the drawdown reduction and of the lower return in strong bull runs.

## 22. Notes stored in results.json
